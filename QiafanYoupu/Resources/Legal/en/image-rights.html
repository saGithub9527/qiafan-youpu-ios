1. Channels for Submitting a Complaint

A logged-in user can use the current image rights complaint page to provide the location of the content involved, a complaint description, contact details, and a signed name as an electronic signature and actively confirm the good-faith and authorization declarations. After saving the page, the user can view their own complaint, processing status, and notes. If you do not have an account or cannot use the entry, you may use the email address above. Login is not a prerequisite for exercising a statutory right.

The current form saves text descriptions and contact details. It cannot automatically determine infringement or guarantee takedown. If an original file or proof of rights is needed, first make contact and submit only what is required for the investigation.

2. Making a Notice Verifiable

  • Identify the content accurately: provide the entry or post name, page link or number, location of the image, and a description that helps identify it.
  • Explain the claim: identify whether it concerns copyright, likeness, privacy, or another right and describe the facts and reasons for believing that right has been infringed.
  • Provide necessary evidence: for example, the source of the original work, publication date, licensing relationship, or authority to act. Preserve the information necessary to establish authenticity and redact unrelated information.
  • Provide valid identity and reply information: give your real name or entity name and a channel where you can receive a response. If applying for someone else, explain the agency relationship. If further verification is needed, the operator will explain the necessary scope.
  • Make the good-faith and authorization declarations: state your good-faith belief that the complained-of use is not permitted by the rights holder or by law; confirm, to the best of your knowledge, that the material is true and accurate; confirm that you are the rights holder or an authorized representative; and accept corresponding responsibility under law for a false notice. Enter your signed name as the electronic signature for this declaration.

Do not provide a complete account token, secret device credential, or unrelated identity documents. If an initial notice is incomplete, the operator should explain what needs to be added and should not use unnecessary formalities to obstruct a complaint.

3. Investigation and Necessary Measures

After receiving a notice, the operator should check the content and submission record involved and, considering the completeness of the material, type of right, risk, and applicable law, take necessary measures such as requesting additions, notifying the uploader, limiting display, deleting, or restoring content. If there is an evident risk to personal safety or privacy, display may be limited first while verification continues.

Handling should be proportionate to the risk. One complaint alone does not automatically establish that a party acted unlawfully, and providing a complaint channel does not release the operator from its duties. “Pending” and “Submitted” are record statuses only; if takedown has not been completed, it must not be described as complete. Staff record the processing status and notes and perform the corresponding acceptance or withdrawal operation for a contributed image. If manual review, contact with a relevant party, or additional material is required, communication occurs through the processing notes or a valid contact channel. Changing the complaint status alone does not automatically delete all complained-of content.

4. The Uploader's Explanation and Appeal

The complained-of party may provide original files, creation or license records, lawful-use grounds, and verifiable contact details. Merely saying that an item was “found online” does not establish permission. If representation or joint rights are involved, explain the relevant relationship.

If a notice or explanation must be forwarded under applicable law, the operator will provide the relevant party only with the claims and evidence necessary to handle the dispute and will limit the dissemination of unrelated personal information. The complaint email address and complete evidence will not be displayed publicly. The need for further material or additional remedies should be clearly stated. If applicable law specifies a procedure for notices, counter-notices, waiting periods, or measures, that procedure applies; these Rules do not create one uniform deadline to replace it.

If the evidence conflicts or the matter is beyond the platform's ability to determine, the parties may negotiate, complain to a competent authority, or bring legal proceedings. Negotiation or a platform appeal is not a prerequisite to exercising statutory remedies.

5. Personal Information and Record Retention

The complaint location, description, contact details, electronic signature, good-faith and authorization declarations, submission time, processing status, and notes are used for investigation, communication, and necessary proof of rights and are not available for lookup by other ordinary users. You may request access to, correction of, or deletion of your information under the Privacy Policy. If statutory retention applies or a dispute has not ended, the reason should be explained and retention and use restricted.

Takedown or withdrawal does not mean that all evidence and underlying media have been physically deleted. Current complaints, contribution statuses, and unused media do not have a uniform automatic expiration and deletion process. Actual retention and backup arrangements are described in the Privacy Policy.

6. Truthful Notices, Repeat Infringement, and Contact

Use the complaint process in good faith. Do not fabricate rights materials or maliciously demand removal of another person's lawful content. For repeated submission of clearly infringing content or abuse of the complaint process, the operator may, based on the facts, risk, and applicable law, impose necessary content or account restrictions and, where practicable, explain the reason and appeal channel. Each party bears responsibility under law for its own conduct.

A general dish name or common cooking method does not mean that another person's photograph or specific original text may be copied freely. The particular rights may also depend on commissioning, work made in the course of employment, likeness rights, trademarks, or privacy and should be determined from the facts.
